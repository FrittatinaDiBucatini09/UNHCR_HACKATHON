## Scope and method

Pages 2 and 5 describe a literature review and key-informant interviews covering **over 25 country operations**, **over 75 documents** and **more than 40 informants**. The focus is household basic-needs assistance, notably multipurpose cash, food and non-food items, with additional sectoral examples. This is a descriptive mapping of approaches, not a randomized evaluation of AI-assisted decision interfaces or a representative beneficiary dataset.

## Key findings to retain

- Pages 7-10: targeting identifies eligible people and selects them for assistance; prioritization addresses needs exceeding available resources. Vulnerability must be defined for the programme and context before measurement. Eligibility criteria and selection methods are separate choices.
- Pages 18-24: large, registered, protracted caseloads often use administrative categorical/PMT approaches; individual scorecards or protection assessments are common when registration data are absent or caseloads smaller. These are mapped observations, explicitly **not recommendations prescribing a universal method** (p. 19).
- Page 24: **implementation exclusion errors** concern incorrect/outdated data or application; **design exclusion errors** concern people with real needs not captured by the criteria or scoring threshold. Reapplying unchanged criteria cannot necessarily solve the second problem.
- Page 24: appeals buffers reserve capacity for additional cases identified through protection assessment or appeals. Budget constraints can prevent adding cases even when the need is identified. No universal buffer percentage is supplied.
- Pages 25-32: institutions have different targeting cultures and objectives; the reported ICRC 80% discussion concerns blanket versus targeted assistance, **not audit sampling**. BRAC selection thresholds are programme-specific historical examples, not Cashy defaults.
- Pages 35-42: national inclusion, national-method alignment and inter-agency harmonization are distinct. Refugees and nationals can use different targeting methods even where coverage or transfer values are aligned.
- Pages 53-54 and 57: Afghanistan's reported approximately 25-minute assessment and Costa Rica's 10-15-minute combined registration/assessment are **different historical tasks**, not benchmarks for Cashy AI-review time.
- Pages 66-68: Morocco combines protection judgments and scorecard evidence with referral, possible further assessment/household visit and panel review. Its reported 74% old/new-method agreement (p. 65 footnote) does not establish either method's accuracy.
- Pages 70-71: the China case describes communication and a seven-day appeal/review path, but this deadline is not a universal UNHCR or Cashy requirement.
