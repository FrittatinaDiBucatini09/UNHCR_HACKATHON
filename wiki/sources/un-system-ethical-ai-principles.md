## Document map

Pages 1-2 explain the purpose, scope, ethical approach, relationship to UNESCO's recommendation and existing UN policies, and implementation needs. Pages 3-4 contain **ten**, not five, complementary principles:

1. Do no harm.
2. Defined purpose, necessity and proportionality.
3. Safety and security.
4. Fairness and non-discrimination.
5. Sustainability.
6. Right to privacy, data protection and data governance.
7. Human autonomy and oversight.
8. Transparency and explainability.
9. Responsibility and accountability.
10. Inclusion and participation.

The introduction applies the framework across the AI lifecycle, including multiple interacting systems. It calls for ethical assessment, governance/risk management, alignment with data-responsibility frameworks, workforce development and practical pilots. These are institutional obligations and guidance, not instructions addressed to this assistant.

## Relevant source claims

- Page 3: an AI method must be justified and proportionate to a legitimate purpose; potential intended and unintended harms must be monitored. Data-subject rights and data integrity require appropriate governance.
- Page 4: oversight must leave meaningful human decision-making, including choosing when/how to use AI and overriding it. Decisions affecting fundamental rights generally require human intervention.
- Page 4: affected individuals should be informed of AI involvement and have understandable access to the reasons and logic behind decisions affecting rights, benefits or services.
- Page 4: accountability requires oversight, impact assessment, audit/due diligence and investigation of harm with appropriate response. The document does **not** specify operator reprimands, a per-case uncertainty formula, review staffing or dashboard time thresholds.
- Page 4: stakeholders and affected communities should participate meaningfully in defining purposes, assumptions, risks and mitigations.
