# Targeting context, exclusion errors and review boundaries

Primary background source: [[sources/unhcr-targeting-and-prioritization-mapping|UNHCR mapping]], research conducted in 2022. Cashy-specific facts come from the [[sources/cashy-oversight-challenge-brief|challenge brief]], not from assuming its unnamed operation is one of the mapping's case studies.

## Logical decision chain

The mapping distinguishes programme objectives and vulnerability definition, eligibility criteria, identification/selection, validation, assistance and continuous monitoring (pp. 7-10). Prioritization becomes necessary where needs exceed resources. Methods may combine status, geography, categorical criteria, expert scorecards, statistical proxy means testing (PMT), community participation and individual protection assessments.

Thus three separate questions must be kept visible:

1. Does the AI agree with the institution's decision/reference under the applicable rule?
2. Does that rule capture the household's actual needs/protection situation?
3. Can the programme provide assistance now, given its resources, or is the household prioritized/wait-listed?

This separation is a project synthesis. An override can correct a prediction without resolving a flawed criterion or an unavailable assistance budget.

## Exclusion taxonomy

| Layer | Example | What review can check |
|---|---|---|
| AI/reference mismatch, Cashy evaluation | AI says exclude, recorded institutional reference says include | Correct override against that reference; not independently established truth about need |
| Implementation exclusion, mapping p. 24 | Outdated household data fail a criterion the household would meet with corrected data | Verify/update evidence and reapply the applicable rule |
| Design exclusion, mapping p. 24 | Household has a real unmet need or protection risk not captured by the criterion/threshold | Protection assessment and authorized exception/programme-policy review, not simply a second identical rule application |
| Resource prioritization constraint | Identified eligible/at-risk household cannot immediately be added to a full assistance list | Document unmet need and escalate resource/programme capacity; do not relabel it as absence of need |

These categories can overlap; the dashboard should not force them into one generic “operator wrong” label. Review/reference protocols must record rule version and contextual constraints. Authority to approve an exception is not established for the Cashy operator and must not be invented.

## Methods and costs are contextual

Pages 18-24 describe administrative methods for many large registered caseloads and resource-intensive individual assessments where data or context require them. The report explicitly says the mapping is descriptive, not a universal prescription (p. 19). This supports considering workload; it does not select the fastest or safest AI-review design.

Afghanistan's approximately 25-minute needs assessment (p. 53) and Costa Rica's 10-15-minute combined registration/assessment (p. 57) concern different historical processes. They do not set a minimum/maximum Cashy review time. Morocco reports longer committee decisions after adding scorecard information (p. 67), illustrating a trade-off rather than a causal estimate applicable to our interface.

## Case-study map for later research

| Example and pages | Method and review relevance |
|---|---|
| Iran, 49-51 | Regular-caseload scorecard versus rapid categorical new-arrival screening; exceptional protection referrals; statistical validation gaps |
| Afghanistan, 52-54 | Geographic prioritization plus multisector scorecard/preconditions; one assessment for multiple interventions reduces duplicated collection |
| Costa Rica, 55-57 | Three vulnerability dimensions, combined registration/assessment, adjustable prioritization, waiting lists and design exclusions |
| Egypt, 58-59 | PMT applied to registration data; appeals/data updating and delayed re-targeting |
| Rwanda, 60-63 | Data analysis plus community-validated categorical criteria; joint appeals panels and feedback into criteria |
| Morocco, 64-68 | Scorecard combined with individual protection judgments, further evidence/visits and panel review; inconsistent prior judgments and residual design exclusions |
| China, 69-71 | Means testing plus protection exceptions, case-based committee review, communicated decisions and appeals |
| Mauritania, 35-37; Cameroon, 38-39 | National-system alignment, different methods/coverage, protection/community validation and appeals capacity |

## Project implications, not adopted settings

Maintain an appeal/protection pathway and distinguish evidence correction from criteria reconsideration. The mapping discusses appeals buffers but supplies no universally valid percentage of cases to audit or number of reviewers. One blind additional reviewer and sampled adjudication remain [[topics/cashy-pipeline-planning|proposed methods]]. An appeals buffer is capacity to add beneficiaries, not the same quantity as an audit sample.

The user's accepted priority is preventing wrongful exclusions generally, **without requiring a separate “grave consequences” gate**. Inclusion errors and resource use still matter; no numerical harm weights have been selected. See [[topics/cashy-manager-monitoring|monitoring]] and [[topics/chat-context|user decisions]].
