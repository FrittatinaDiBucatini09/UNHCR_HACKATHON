# Cashy ethical guardrails and source boundaries

Primary normative source: [[sources/un-system-ethical-ai-principles|UN-system AI principles]], pages 1-4. Participant-facing requirements remain separately documented in [[topics/cashy-submission|challenge requirements]]. The table's third column is a **project interpretation to investigate**, not a validated intervention or an adopted operational policy.

| Principle | Source meaning, summarized | Possible project implication |
|---|---|---|
| Do no harm | Avoid and monitor intended/unintended individual and collective harm | Track exclusions and inclusion/resource impacts; investigate failures, including criteria failures |
| Defined purpose, necessity and proportionality | Justify AI methods within mandate and context | Do not add an uncertainty-prediction model solely to obtain an impressive-looking percentage |
| Safety and security | Identify and mitigate lifecycle risks | Define access, failure handling and safe review/escalation paths |
| Fairness and non-discrimination | Distribute benefits/risks/costs justly, prevent discrimination and stigma | Inspect subgroup impacts where lawful, meaningful and supported; avoid operator stigmatization from unsupported metrics |
| Sustainability | Assess social, economic and environmental effects | Evaluate ongoing review workload, maintainability and resources |
| Privacy, data protection and governance | Protect rights and data integrity | Minimize identifiable data, restrict access and retain an appropriate audit trail |
| Human autonomy and oversight | Meaningful choice and ability to override; human intervention for rights-affecting decisions | Assess whether the operator can actually verify, contest and escalate, not merely click approval |
| Transparency and explainability | Traceable decisions and understandable reasons for affected people | Distinguish true decision evidence from a persuasive LLM narrative; test accessible household-facing communication |
| Responsibility and accountability | Oversight, audit, impact assessment, investigation and response | Give managers traceable cases and review responsibilities; no source mandates automatic employee sanctions |
| Inclusion and participation | Meaningful consultation with stakeholders and affected communities | Treat households as participants in criteria, feedback and recourse, not only recipients of a justification |

## Governance is not an assurance banner

The [[topics/cashy-experiment|Cashy study]] tested generic on-screen statements drawn from five principles. The UN document contains ten. The reported reduction in correct overrides concerns that experiment's assurance treatment; it is not a reason to remove actual transparency, accountability or oversight. Source prescriptions and empirical intervention effects answer different questions.

The two uploaded PDFs do not determine whether to show AI first, require a preliminary independent judgment or present guided checks before AI. Those variants remain for the [[topics/cashy-pipeline-planning|literature review and evaluation]]; no interface has been selected.

## Manager-only indicators and procedural transparency

The user proposes that a per-decision uncertainty/review indicator be visible only to the manager. Restricting display of an internal triage flag is not a decision to hide AI involvement, eligibility reasons or recourse from the affected household. Source page 4 requires understandable information about rights-affecting AI-informed decisions. Access roles, staff awareness of monitoring, retention and household communication require separate design decisions.
