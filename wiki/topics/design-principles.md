# Design principles

This page preserves the programme's explicit design rationale and synthesizes the progression across the three days.

## Day 0 - shared frame

The public opening creates a shared frame before the hackathon. By linking current uses of AI in humanitarian contexts with research on vulnerable populations, participants are invited to see technology as embedded in real **social, ethical, and operational challenges**.

The high-stakes questions named explicitly are:

- fairness;
- transparency;
- explainability;
- human oversight.

## Day 1 morning - people and context before solutions

The morning deliberately progresses from:

1. institutional framing;
2. lived experience;
3. data exploration.

The aim is to keep **people, context, and operational consequences visible** before teams accelerate into solution-building. The schedule implements this through opening remarks, challenge reveal, voices of students with lived experience of forced displacement, hands-on data exploration, and a Q&A on context, data, constraints, and responsible use.

## Day 1 afternoon and evening - experiment, test, reconnect

The afternoon moves from understanding to experimentation. Multidisciplinary teams translate disciplinary perspectives into a first solution while mentoring and checkpoints help them:

- test assumptions;
- challenge early ideas;
- assess feasibility;
- progressively define a shared direction.

The evening consolidates both the work and the team. The informal dinner, feedback, pitch guidance, and final sprint are intended to help participants step away from isolated tasks, reconnect around a common vision, and prepare a focused plan for the final day.

## Day 2 - accountable proposition

The morning moves from an initial idea to a more robust and coherent solution. Teams use evidence, mentor feedback, and critical reflection to:

- refine the prototype;
- examine limitations;
- keep technical choices connected to humanitarian needs.

The final afternoon converts the prototype into a clear and accountable proposition. Teams must not only demonstrate what they built, but also explain:

- why it matters;
- how it could be used responsibly;
- where its limitations remain.

Final presentations and awards value both **solution quality** and the **learning process** behind it.

## Cross-day synthesis

The programme's logic is:

**shared ethical frame -> lived experience -> contextualized data -> multidisciplinary problem framing -> mentored experimentation -> assumption testing -> evidence-based refinement -> explainability and impact framing -> accountable pitch**.

This synthesis is an interpretation of the explicit design-principle passages, not an additional claim by the organizers.
